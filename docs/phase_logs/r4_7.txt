      ratio   = 5.80 (indicates moderate tet aspect ratio variation / slivers)

Physical and Discretisation Causes:
  1. Flux Discretisation:
     FEM represents temperature using continuous piecewise linear C0 nodal basis
     functions. The boundary Neumann flux is distributed variationally:
       b_i = ∫ q N_i dA = q A / 3
     This spreads the thermal flux smoothly over all tetrahedra sharing each boundary
     vertex. In contrast, cell-centered FVM deposits the entire boundary face load
     directly into the single adjacent cell:
       b_P += q * A
     For cells with smaller volume (the 6-tet-per-cube decomposition creates tets
     with varying volume ratios up to 5.8×), the effective volumetric heating
     rate q*A/V_P is higher, producing a localized cell-centered peak.
  2. Cell-Centered vs Nodal Sampling:
     FEM maximum occurs at the peak boundary node. FVM maximum occurs at a cell
     centroid near the boundary. Because FVM uses piecewise-constant cell values,
     steep thermal gradients near the 10×10 mm source patch edge produce a slight
     cell-average overshoot relative to nodal linear interpolation.
  3. Away from the immediate boundary flux discontinuity, interior probe points
     and volume-weighted average temperatures (Tavg) agree within 0.04 °C between
     FEM and FVM (FEM Fine Tavg = 56.5700 °C, FVM Fine Tavg = 56.6084 °C).
